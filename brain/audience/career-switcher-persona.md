**Who this course is for.** A career-switcher — or a student on the same path — who wants to build and judge web
pages, and intends the skill to lead to work. They are the persona for the Web Design & Development course, second
of the three named in v1 planning. They are not the salesperson of `salesperson-persona`: that learner is made
conversant about a system someone else builds, while this one means to produce. A lesson that blurs the two has
left the persona.

**What they already know.** They have used the web for years and hold opinions about pages. Many have touched a
site builder or a design tool, so they know dragging an element changes a page — but not that a page is a set of
rules applied to a structure. They recognise common tags by sight and can copy a snippet without knowing why it
works.

**Weekly time budget.** Four weeks, two to three hours a week, in evenings around a job or study. A lesson that
needs an uninterrupted hour does not get read.

**Motivation, and the honest limit.** They are here for employability, which makes them impatient for something to
show. The platform grades no visual artefact, so this course makes them able to decide and defend a layout, type,
contrast and responsive decision — not to certify a portfolio. Saying so in week one is part of the teaching;
letting them discover it in week three is how we lose them.

**What they carry into the room.** That CSS is a paint program and a page is a picture. That a visual fix is a
local fix. That `!important` is a solution rather than a symptom. That you can style your way out of a wrong
structure. That "can you just move it a bit?" is a small request.

**How they fail.** They quit when the course reads as theory with nothing to build; or they mistake tool fluency
for a model of the page, and cannot say why their layout broke when the screen narrowed.

**Tone this implies.** Concrete and page-first: show the page, then name what governs it. Never precious about
design vocabulary — a term earns its place by making a decision easier to defend.
