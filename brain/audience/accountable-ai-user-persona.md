**Who this course is for.** Someone who has to *produce work* with an AI assistant and put their own name on the result, with no prior AI experience and no intention of writing code. They are the spine persona for the Claude Fundamentals course, and — because nobody can delegate to a tool they cannot judge — the entry persona every career pathway starts from.

**What separates them from our other personas.** The salesperson and general-beginner personas are made *conversant and correctly-modelled* about systems other people build. This learner is different in kind: they are **accountable for output they cannot fully verify**. Everything the course does follows from that one pressure — briefing well, noticing invention, and knowing which jobs to refuse are all judgement skills, not knowledge.

**What they already know.** How to brief a colleague, chase a supplier, judge whether a draft is ready to send. They have probably typed something into an assistant and been impressed and burned in the same session. They have no model of *why* either happened.

**What they will never need.** Prompt folklore, model benchmarks, API code, anything measured in months of shelf life. The course makes them capable and appropriately sceptical, never a builder.

**What they carry into the room (the misconceptions to pre-empt).** That it looked the answer up. That confidence signals correctness. That it remembers them. That asking "are you sure?" is a check. That a tool which handled something hard can handle something easy. Each is a real belief this learner arrives with, and each is the misconception paragraph of a lesson.

**Where they will use it.** The same week, on their own work — attaching the source instead of trusting recall, or telling a colleague that the payroll total is not a job for this. The test of a lesson is whether it changes what they do on Monday, not whether they can define a term.

**Tone this implies.** Practical and non-condescending. They are competent adults who are new to one specific tool, which is not the same as beginners at their job. Never imply the machine is smarter than them; the whole course rests on their judgement outranking its fluency.
