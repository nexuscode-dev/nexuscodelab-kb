**Who this course is for.** An engineer who reads and writes code, now hands substantial implementation to an AI coding tool, and merges the result under their own name. Spine persona for `ai-era-engineering-judgment`, and the AI-Era Software Engineering pathway's first persona that is *not* its entry persona.

**What separates them from [[accountable-ai-user-persona]].** That learner is accountable for output they **cannot** verify, so their course teaches scepticism and which jobs to refuse. This learner **can** verify — read the diff, run the tests, reason about the failure path. The pressure is different in kind: AI output arrives faster, larger and more uniformly polished than their review habits were built for. The risk is not trust in a black box; it is a review process calibrated on human code meeting output that produces no human signals.

**What they already know.** A language, version control, tests, how to review a colleague's pull request. They have shipped something, and merged AI-written code that looked fine.

**What they will never need here.** Prompt folklore, model internals, benchmarks, building agents, anything with months of shelf life. The course makes them a better delegator and reviewer, not a tool builder.

**What they carry into the room (the misconceptions to pre-empt).** That the work to delegate is the boring work. That passing tests mean correct code. That reviewing AI output is the same activity as reviewing a colleague's. That a clean skim means a clean diff. That a fresh generation beats a correction. That one more round will land it. That a bug in AI-written code is a different class of mistake. Each is held by competent engineers, and each is a lesson's misconception paragraph.

**Where they will use it.** In their next review of a diff they did not write: choosing where to spend twenty minutes, writing the case the generator would not have thought of, and saying "I will write this one myself" three rounds in.

**Tone this implies.** Peer to peer. The tool changed their job; nothing here teaches them to code, and nothing implies it outranks their judgement. Concrete situations over principles: this learner is convinced by a diff, not by advice.
