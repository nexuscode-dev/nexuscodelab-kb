**Who this course is for.** A salesperson with no technical background who sells, scopes, or supports software and needs to understand how a web system works at an overview level. They are the spine persona for v1 (the web-system-architecture course), chosen because they are the audience the team named most specifically.

**What they already know.** They are fluent in the *business* around software: clients, requirements, timelines, "can we just add a field?" conversations. They are comfortable with jargon as vocabulary — they have heard "API", "backend", "database", "the server" — but the words are labels, not models. They can hold an analogy and reason from it.

**What they will never need.** Syntax of any language, any framework by name, any cloud provider or its pricing, how to write a query, how to deploy. This course makes them *conversant and correctly-modelled*, not able to build. A lesson that drifts toward "here is how you would code it" has left the persona.

**What they carry into the room (the misconceptions to pre-empt).** That the browser "has" the data. That frontend is looks and backend is logic. That "just add a field" is a small change. That a slow page means a slow internet connection. Each of these is a real belief a salesperson brings, and each maps to a domain note whose whole job is to replace it.

**Where they will use it.** The week after the lesson, in a client or delivery conversation — "no, that change touches the database structure, it is not a text edit." The test of a lesson is whether it survives being repeated, out loud, by someone who is not technical, to someone who is.

**Tone this implies.** Respectful of their intelligence, ruthless about jargon. They are smart and busy; they will not forgive a lesson that wastes their time proving how much the author knows.
