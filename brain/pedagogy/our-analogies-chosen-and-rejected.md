**Rule.** One house analogy per core concept, recorded once, not reinvented per lesson — with the analogies we reject and why.

**Why.** A salesperson reasons from the analogy long after the lesson; two pictures for one concept build two shaky models.

**How we choose.** It must carry the concept's *boundary* and survive translation to Japanese (`every-analogy-must-survive-japanese`). If it implies something false when pushed one step, reject it.

**The house choices (and rejected ones).**

- **Server = bank teller, not waiter.** Checks who you are, fetches from a vault you cannot enter. Rejected: *waiter/restaurant* — implies queueing and politeness, and learners over-extend it.
- **Frontend/backend = a trust line, not two job titles.** Rejected: *"frontend is looks, backend is logic"* — a description, not a boundary.
- **Schema change = renovating a building, not editing a document.** Rejected: *"add a column like a spreadsheet column"* — hides that structure and data differ.
- **Browser rendering = a projector showing a slide (L1).** The slide comes from elsewhere; the picture is produced locally.
- **A request = a posted letter (L6).** Post it, wait for the reply to travel back — the round trip.
- **Latency vs bandwidth = the delivery van carrying the L6 letter (L7).** How long the van takes vs how big it is. Always composed with the posted letter.
- **Status codes (4xx vs 5xx — whose fault) = the marking on a returned letter (L8).** Extends the L6 letter: "no such address" is the sender's fault (request side); "sorting office on fire" is the postal service's fault (server side). A *return* marking, not a postage stamp (in Japanese, a returned-mail marking, never 切手). The old envelope-vs-contents picture is retired here, reserved for headers/body in a future course.
- **Session / session ID = a cloakroom ticket (L12).** The attendant reads the ticket you present, not your face.
- **API = a vending machine's button panel (L16).** A fixed set of allowed requests: press B4, get exactly that; nothing off-panel. Rejected: *restaurant menu* — drifts toward negotiation.
- **Third-party service = the electricity grid (L17).** Rent power from a specialist rather than build a plant — essential, not your business.
- **Scaling = opening more checkout lanes (L18).** Add lanes and direct customers to a free one, not one enormous cashier.

**The misconception this pre-empts.** A wrong analogy is worse than none: it must be un-taught later.
