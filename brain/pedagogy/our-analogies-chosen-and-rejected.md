**Rule.** One house analogy per core concept, chosen on purpose, with the analogies we reject and why. An analogy is a teaching decision, recorded once, not reinvented per lesson.

**Why.** A salesperson reasons from the analogy long after the lesson; two pictures for one concept build two shaky models. Consistency beats a locally cuter comparison.

**How we choose.** It must carry the concept's *boundary* and survive translation to Japanese (`every-analogy-must-survive-japanese`). If it implies something false when pushed one step, reject it — beginners always push it one step.

**The house choices (and rejected ones).**

- **Server = bank teller, not waiter.** Checks who you are, fetches from a vault you cannot enter. Rejected: *waiter/restaurant* — implies queueing and politeness, and learners over-extend it.
- **Frontend/backend = a trust line, not two job titles.** Rejected: *"frontend is looks, backend is logic"* — a description, not a boundary; collapses once validation appears in both.
- **Schema change = renovating a building, not editing a document.** Rejected: *"add a column like a spreadsheet column"* — hides that structure and data differ.
- **Browser rendering = a projector showing a slide (L1).** The slide comes from elsewhere; the picture is produced locally.
- **A request = a posted letter (L6).** Post it, wait for the reply to travel back — the round trip.
- **Latency vs bandwidth = a delivery van (L7).** How long it takes to arrive vs how big it is. Always paired with the letter.
- **A request's outside vs its contents = an envelope and its stamp (L8).** Outer markings, including the status stamp, are separate from what is inside.
- **Session / session ID = a cloakroom ticket (L12).** The attendant reads the ticket you present, not your face; identity travels with each request.
- **API = a vending machine's button panel (L16).** A fixed set of allowed requests: press B4, get what B4 gives; nothing off-panel, never reach inside. Rejected: *restaurant menu* — drifts toward negotiation, not a fixed contract.
- **Third-party service = the electricity grid (L17).** Rent power from a specialist rather than build a plant — essential, not your business.
- **Scaling = opening more checkout lanes (L18).** Add lanes with someone directing customers to a free one, not one enormous cashier.

**The misconception this pre-empts.** That any vivid comparison will do. A wrong analogy is worse than none: it must be un-taught later.
